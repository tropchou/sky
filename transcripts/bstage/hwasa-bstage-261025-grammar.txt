=== [8:21] 기다려봐 와이파이 키고 올게 ===
meaning: Wait, I'll go turn on the Wi-Fi and come back.
tone: Casual 반말, playful but firm; she is addressing chat before going to fix the connection.
--- word ---
term: 기다려봐
type: verb (casual imperative)
breakdown: 기다리다 + -어 보다 + -아/어 (기다려 + 봐)
meaning: wait (and see)
notes: Casual 반말 command. -어 봐 softens a command to 'give it a try', so 기다려봐 is like 'hang on a sec'. Polite version: 기다려 보세요 (please wait). Often written 기다려 봐 with a space.
--- word ---
term: 와이파이
type: noun
breakdown: 
meaning: Wi-Fi
notes: English loanword. Koreans say it as 와이파이 (wai-pa-i). Casual and standard everywhere.
--- word ---
term: 키고
type: verb (connective)
breakdown: 켜다 + -고 (standard: 켜고)
meaning: turn on, and then
notes: 키다 is a very common spoken mistake for 켜다 (to turn on a device or light). Correct spelling is 켜고. Do not confuse with 끄다 (to turn off). -고 here links two actions in sequence: turn on and then come.
--- word ---
term: 올게
type: verb ending
breakdown: 오다 + -ㄹ게
meaning: I'll come (back)
notes: -ㄹ게 expresses a promise or decision made toward the listener, casual 반말. Polite: 올게요. Here 오다 (to come) means she will come back after turning it on. Do not use -ㄹ게 for questions or for other people's actions.
--- grammar ---
pattern: V-고 올게
meaning: I'll do V and come back (a short errand then return).
example: 커피 사고 올게. (I'll buy coffee and come back.)
level: Beginner
--- grammar ---
pattern: V-아/어 봐
meaning: Try doing V; softens a casual command.
example: 먹어 봐. (Try eating it.)
level: Beginner

=== [8:24] 금방 해. 정말이야. ===
meaning: I'll be quick. Really.
tone: Casual, reassuring; promising chat she'll be fast.
--- word ---
term: 금방
type: adverb
breakdown: 
meaning: right away, in a moment
notes: Means shortly or very soon, about the near future or something quick. Similar: 곧 (soon, a bit more formal) and 바로 (immediately). Very common in spoken Korean.
--- word ---
term: 해
type: verb ending
breakdown: 하다 + -아/어 (해)
meaning: I'll do (it)
notes: Present tense casual 반말 form. With 금방 it carries a near-future promise: 'I'll do it quickly.' Polite: 해요. Context shows she means doing the Wi-Fi task quickly.
--- word ---
term: 정말이야
type: noun + copula ending
breakdown: 정말 + 이다 + -야 (이야)
meaning: it's true, really
notes: 정말 means 'truly' and 정말이야 means 'it's the truth'. Casual 반말 for emphasizing sincerity. Alternatives: 진짜야 (more colloquial, 'for real') and 정말이에요 (polite).
--- grammar ---
pattern: N + 이야/야
meaning: casual 반말 copula 'it is N'; 이야 after a consonant-ending noun and 야 after a vowel-ending noun.
example: 진짜야. (It's real.)
level: Beginner

=== [8:38] 안 꺼지네? ===
meaning: It's not cutting out, huh?
tone: Casual, relieved; noticing the connection is holding.
--- word ---
term: 안
type: adverb
breakdown: 
meaning: not
notes: Negative adverb placed before a verb, as in 안 가다 (not go). Short-form negation is common in speech. Alternative is the long form -지 않다.
--- word ---
term: 꺼지네
type: verb ending
breakdown: 꺼지다 + -네
meaning: it turns off / cuts out, I see
notes: 꺼지다 means to go off (a light or device) and here, per the live, the stream dropping. Its opposite is 켜지다 (to come on). -네 expresses a new realization or mild surprise, casual 반말. Here 안 꺼지네 means 'huh, it's not cutting out.' With a rising tone it becomes a question seeking confirmation. Do not confuse 끄다 (to turn something off, transitive) with 꺼지다 (to go off by itself).
--- grammar ---
pattern: V-네
meaning: Expresses new discovery or mild surprise about what you just noticed.
example: 비가 오네. (Oh, it's raining.)
level: Beginner

=== [8:42] 어때? 이제 괜찮지? ===
meaning: How is it? It's fine now, right?
tone: Casual, checking in with chat; seeking confirmation after the fix.
--- word ---
term: 어때?
type: adjective ending (question)
breakdown: 어떠하다 → 어때
meaning: How is it?
notes: Contraction of 어떠해 (어떻다, 'to be how'). Casual 반말 question for asking opinions or the state of things. Polite: 어때요? or 어떠세요?. Often used to ask 'what do you think?'
--- word ---
term: 이제
type: adverb
breakdown: 
meaning: now, by now
notes: Marks a change of state: 'now (that things have changed)'. Compared with 지금 (right now, the present moment), 이제 implies a before and after. Here it means 'now that I've fixed it.'
--- word ---
term: 괜찮지?
type: adjective ending (tag question)
breakdown: 괜찮다 + -지
meaning: it's okay, right?
notes: -지 with rising intonation seeks agreement, like English 'right?' or 'isn't it?'. 괜찮다 means okay or fine. Casual 반말; polite is 괜찮죠?. Common learner confusion: -지 is not just 'doesn't it' but assumes the listener shares the knowledge.
--- grammar ---
pattern: A/V-지?
meaning: Tag question asking for confirmation, 'right?'
example: 맛있지? (It's tasty, right?)
level: Beginner
