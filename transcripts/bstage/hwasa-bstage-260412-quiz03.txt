# Answer Key

**Q1 — A)** Admits that it's true, often hinting at a 'but'
V/A-기는 V/A repeats the word to concede: 'it IS good (but...)'. It is not a promise or a question.

**Q2 — B)** 거든
"너무 좋거든?" = 'I love it, you know?' -거든 adds the reason or background that the listener may not know; 잖아 would assume shared knowledge.

**Q3 — A)** It is too honest for Twitter, where she wants something more infuriating
She says she loves it but it is "a bit too honest" (너무 정직해), since on Twitter she wants something infuriating and cute. It is not rejected for length or for being taken.

**Q4 — D)** honest
정직하다 = to be honest; she means the title is too straightforwardly revealing.

**Q5 — B)** Reminds the listener of something they already know
-잖아 is 'you know / as you know'. Surprise at a new discovery would be -네.

**Q6 — B)** 네
"나혜진이 전통이네" = ''Na Hye-jin' is a tradition!' -네 expresses a realization.

**Q7 — C)** It has a culture and a history, though she cannot remember how it started
She says it is a tradition with culture and history, then admits she doesn't even remember how it began (기억도 안난다).

**Q8 — B)** to be annoyed or infuriated (playfully)
열받다 = get infuriated; here it is used playfully about the cuteness.

**Q9 — A)** 가
"포인트가 있어" = 'there is a point'. 있다 takes the subject marker 가 after a vowel-ending noun; 를 would make it an object.

**Q10 — C)** Keep the chosen tag secret and use it only among themselves
V-지 말고 V-자 means 'let's not share it anywhere, let's use it just among us.' She wants a secret password-like tag.

**Q11 — A)** 겠네
"미치겠네" = 'this is driving me crazy.' -겠네 is an exclamation of how one feels about the situation; the others change the meaning.

**Q12 — A)** 후보
"일단 이삭토순이 후보" = 'for now, candidate: Isak-tosuni'. 후보 = candidate.

**Q13 — A)** 도
"토순이에서도 좀 벗어나봐" = 'step away from Tosuni a bit too.' 도 means 'also/even' after 에서; the others make no sense here.

**Q14 — C)** Realization of something just noticed
-구나 marks a newly noticed fact: 'oh, you like Tosuni.' It is not a suggestion.

**Q15 — A)** They can only think of toast, hinting at Isaac Toast
Isak-tosuni is a pun on Isaac Toast, so she teases them for that.

**Q16 — C)** Their Tosuni suggestions all sound like toast, as in Isaac Toast
Fans keep pushing Tosuni, which echoes Isaac Toast, so she asks if toast is all they can think of.

**Q17 — B)** 까
"이삭토순이로 갈까?" = 'Shall we go with Isak-tosuni?' -ㄹ까 proposes or asks.

**Q18 — C)** 잖아
"바꾸면 되잖아" = 'we can just change it, right?' -잖아 reminds the listener of the obvious.

**Q19 — C)** sticks or clings (in the mind)
달라붙다 = to stick; the catchy name sticks in her head.

**Q20 — D)** She holds a small vote between candidates, and Isak-tosuni clearly wins
She runs a mini vote with Isak-tosuni as number 1 and says it is in a league of its own (독보적이군), then decides on it.

**Q21 — D)** 투표
"투표 약간의 투표를 해볼게요" = 'a vote, let me do a little vote'. 투표 = vote.

**Q22 — B)** Il est hors concours.
독보적 = unrivaled; -군 shows noticing, so 'hors concours'.

**Q23 — D)** 결정
"이삭토스니로 결정" = 'decided: Isak-tosuni'.

**Q24 — B)** forget (casual)
까먹다 is a casual word for forget, more colloquial than 잊다.

**Q25 — B)** 돼
This is a trick-free check of the pattern; the correct word is 돼 from -(으)면 되다.

**Q26 — B)** 검색창
"검색창에다가 이삭토순이 치면 돼" = 'just type it in the search bar'.

**Q27 — D)** Wondering whether to try doing something
-어 보다 = try, -ㄹ까 = shall I. Together: 'should I try making one?'

**Q28 — D)** 계정
"비밀 계정을 하나 만들어 볼까" = 'should I make a secret account'.

**Q29 — A)** feels like pressure
부담스럽다 means burdensome or pressuring.

**Q30 — D)** 게
"눈팅을 할게" = 'I'll just lurk.' -ㄹ게 expresses a promise or decision made toward the listener.

**Q31 — A)** Je vais regarder en silence, sans poster.
눈팅 = lurking, as in the story bible.

**Q32 — C)** How can she show that she stopped by and looked?
She will lurk, so she wonders how to leave a sign that she visited.

**Q33 — C)** The speaker's promise or decision toward the listener
-(으)ㄹ게 commits the speaker: 'I'll think about it.' It is not a guess.

**Q34 — C)** carefully and thoughtfully
신중하다 = careful and thoughtful.

**Q35 — A)** 잖아
"치면 되는 거잖아" = 'I just type it, right?' -잖아 seeks confirmation of something obvious.

**Q36 — C)** Echoes a viewer's question back: 'You're asking...?'
-냐고 repeats someone's question. She is reacting to a chat comment.

**Q37 — B)** 정확하게
"더 정확하게 뜨지" = 'shows up more accurately'.

**Q38 — D)** Sorting by latest will probably give more accurate results
She relays that viewing by latest (최신순) is likely more accurate; she also repeated that the # must be attached without a space.

**Q39 — B)** Repost everything from the tag without permission
불펌 means reposting without permission; she jokingly says she will do it, and also download everything illegally.

**Q40 — A)** by chance
우연히 = by chance or accidentally.

**Q41 — D)** That the fans are some weird group
She imagines strangers clicking the tag and thinking it is some odd group (이상한 단체), or that Hwasa models for Isaac Toast.

**Q42 — C)** quietly or subtly, more than you'd think
은근히 = subtly; there are low-key plenty of talented people.

**Q43 — D)** 도
"멍청이들 중에서도" = 'even among the Twits'. 도 adds 'even'; the other particles do not fit after 중에서.

**Q44 — B)** Really useless, trivial, small and cute things
She says she likes all useless and trivial stuff, the small, precious and cute things are the best.

**Q45 — C)** Dramatic, old-fashioned call (mock-epic)
(이)여 is a literary vocative; she uses it theatrically for comedy.

**Q46 — A)** Allez-y, partez !
-거라 is an old-fashioned command: 'go forth'.

**Q47 — A)** In mock anger, she wants whoever posted her belly pic to come forward
She is playfully furious, calling out the poster and telling them to run over, while still being curious to see the tag.

**Q48 — B)** Mock-angry, affectionate scolding
It is a fake-furious scolding of the fans (little brats), clearly playful.

**Q49 — D)** 어
"보고 싶어" = 'I want to see.' The ending 어 completes the casual style; the others are particles that do not fit.

**Q50 — D)** Formal military-style command used jokingly
-(하)라 is a formal command, used humorously like a general.
