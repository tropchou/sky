# Answer Key

**Q1 — C)** She has a bad cold.
감기가 심하게 들어서 means 'I have a bad cold, so...', and the next line says her breathing might be loud. Nothing in the lines mentions running or sleepiness.

**Q2 — B)** A prediction about what will probably happen
'내 숨소리가 좀 시끄러울 거야' means 'my breathing might be a bit loud'. -(으)ㄹ 거야 is a guess about the near future, not a promise.

**Q3 — D)** 콧물
'근데 이렇게 콧물 맛이' means 'But this taste of a runny nose...'. Only 콧물 (snot) fits a 'taste' she is joking about.

**Q4 — A)** Wondering or worrying to herself about something uncertain
She has just said 'I'm worried', so '내일 괜찮을까' is a self-directed 'will I be okay tomorrow?'. It is not an invitation.

**Q5 — C)** 나 봐
'너무 많이 먹었나 봐' means 'I think I ate too much'. -나 봐 is a guess from the situation. The other endings do not fit after 먹었.

**Q6 — D)** 을
'홍콩 와플을 먹고 왔어' means 'I went and ate a Hong Kong waffle'. 와플 ends in a consonant, so the object particle is 을, not 를. 이 and 에서 do not mark the thing eaten.

**Q7 — A)** She is trying to recall something she experienced or knew before
'와플 어디였더라 이름이' means 'Where was that waffle place... what was its name?'. -더라 is about recalling a past experience or fact.

**Q8 — A)** 거든
'에그타르트 매니저님이 사다 주셨거든' means 'the egg tart my manager bought for me, you see...'. -거든 gives background or a reason. The other options do not fit after 주셨.

**Q9 — B)** 야지
'잘 먹어야지' means 'I have to eat well'. -어야지 expresses a resolution or obligation to oneself. The other forms leave the sentence incomplete.

**Q10 — C)** 에서
'그래서 부랴부랴 공항에서 키트를 사서' means 'So I rushed to buy a kit at the airport'. 에서 marks the place where an action happens. 에게 is only for people.

**Q11 — B)** It doesn't seem to be COVID, but might be the flu
She says it doesn't seem to be COVID, and in the next line that she thinks it might be the flu. She never says she tested positive.

**Q12 — D)** It reminds the listener of something they should already know
'걸렸었잖아' means 'I had it before, you know'. -잖아 appeals to shared knowledge.

**Q13 — A)** to catch (an illness)
With an illness like 코로나 or 감기, 걸리다 means 'to catch'. The 'take time' meaning exists but does not fit here.

**Q14 — C)** advantage and disadvantage
장점 is a strength or advantage and 단점 is a weakness or disadvantage. -(이)자 joins them: 'both a pro and a con'.

**Q15 — A)** Quel est le rapport avec mon emploi du temps ?
무슨 상관이야 means 'what does that have to do with it?', so the whole line asks about the link with her schedule.

**Q16 — D)** 한테
'그래서 그거 좀 언니들한테 사다 달라 했어' means 'So I asked the unnis to buy some for me'. 한테 marks the person being asked. 을 would make the unnis the thing bought.

**Q17 — C)** 에서
'약국에서 사 먹어 본 감기약' means 'cold medicine I bought at a pharmacy and tried'. 에서 marks the place of the action. 에게 is for people.

**Q18 — B)** 누웠는데
'내가 이렇게 침대를 누웠는데' means 'I lay down on the bed like this...'. The other verbs do not make sense with 침대를 here.

**Q19 — D)** 잊다 is the neutral 'forget / let slip from memory'; 까먹다 is casual and colloquial
Both mean 'forget'. 까먹다 is everyday spoken language, as in #604, while 잊다 sounds a little more neutral or literary, as in 'I can never forget that moment'.

**Q20 — A)** 는 줄
'침대가 나를 삼키는 줄 알았어' means 'I thought the bed was swallowing me'. -는 줄 알았다 is 'thought that... (but it wasn't)'.

**Q21 — A)** The sinking feeling when she lay down after taking NyQuil
She took NyQuil, lay down right after the meeting, and felt the bed swallow her up. She compares it to the sinking scene in Get Out.

**Q22 — A)** After finishing the action
-고 나서 means 'after having done'. The line means 'after the bed swallowed me', and she woke up the next day.

**Q23 — C)** Like she woke up with her brain washed clean
She says that for the first time in years she felt as if her brain had been washed clean. That is why she became such a believer in NyQuil.

**Q24 — D)** Coming to be in a state, ending up that way
맹신하게 됐다 means 'I came to believe blindly'. -게 되다 describes a change that happened over time or by circumstance.

**Q25 — A)** probably / after all
아무래도 means 'in all likelihood' and introduces her best guess about where something was. 갑자기 is 'suddenly' and 심지어 is 'even'.

**Q26 — B)** 영양제
'내가 요새 영양제를 안 먹게 됐다' means 'I haven't been taking supplements lately'. You can 먹다 a supplement, but not jet lag, stamina or a lie.

**Q27 — D)** to pack / bring along and look after
챙겨가다 is 'to take along', i.e. make sure to have it with her. It fits her plan to take Vitamin C every morning.

**Q28 — C)** 가
'나 브라질 갔다 와서 시차가 계속 엉망이더라고' means 'My jet lag has been a mess since I got back from Brazil'. 시차 is the subject of 엉망이다, so it takes 이/가. 시차 ends in a vowel, so 가.

**Q29 — D)** to suit / fit well
With 시차 and 잘, 맞다 means the time difference suited her body. It is not 'to be right' or 'to be hit'.

**Q30 — B)** Readjusting to Korean time after Brazil
Brazil's time zone suited her, but 적응 (adjusting) back in Korea was hard. She is talking about jet lag.

**Q31 — C)** She was bored
심심해서 means 'because I was bored'. She says she did it out of boredom while very tired.

**Q32 — D)** 갑자기
'그니까 나도 갑자기 라이브를 왜 한 거야' means 'Why did I suddenly do a live stream?'. 갑자기 is 'suddenly'. 심지어 means 'even' and doesn't fit the question.

**Q33 — B)** even / what's more (adds an extreme point)
심지어 adds a more surprising item: 'I even couldn't eat breakfast'. It pairs naturally with 도 ('even').

**Q34 — B)** A noun-style ending used in written comments or notes
Ending a sentence in -(으)ㅁ gives a short, note-like style common in online comments. It is not a speech level, and it's the fan's words, not hers.

**Q35 — A)** 거짓말
'거짓말 치지 마' means 'Don't lie'. The set phrase is 거짓말(을) 치다. The other nouns can't be paired with 치다.

**Q36 — C)** sold out
품절 means an item is out of stock. She asks whether the chestnut snacks are sold out.

**Q37 — B)** Because she herself bought them all
사와서 그래 means 'it's because I bought them (and brought them)'. She jokes that she bought them all.

**Q38 — A)** 니까
'당연히 더우니까 하면서 가디건을 벗고 갔다' means 'I took off my cardigan, thinking "of course it'll be hot"'. -(으)니까 gives a reason, which is her thought at the time. The other options don't fit that logic.

**Q39 — A)** She took it off expecting heat, but it was cold
She assumed it would be hot in Hong Kong summer and left her cardigan, then she was freezing (추워가지고 혼났네).

**Q40 — B)** J'avais si froid que j'ai vraiment dégusté.
혼나다 literally means 'to be scolded', but here it means 'to have a rough time'. The line says she was so cold she suffered.

**Q41 — A)** It was a heart-shaped tissue design
She first thought she got snot on the tissue, then saw it was a heart. She calls it a 하트 휴지 (heart tissue).

**Q42 — B)** 네
'콧물인 줄 알았네' means 'I thought it was snot'. -네 shows surprise at a new discovery. The other endings don't fit.

**Q43 — D)** 볼게
'에휴 휴지 좀 꺼내볼게' means 'Sigh. Let me get out some tissues'. -(으)ㄹ게 announces what she will do, and 보다 adds 'try'. The other options give the wrong meaning.

**Q44 — C)** Je suis dans de beaux draps, c'est la catastrophe.
큰일 나다 means 'a big problem has come up'. She says it worrying about her stuffy nose for tomorrow's concert.

**Q45 — B)** Le sort en est jeté.
It is the Korean version of 'the die is cast': a decision has been made and can't be undone.

**Q46 — C)** Once you start short hair, you can't do other styles; it's hard to grow out
She says that once you start with a short cut you can't do other hairstyles, so she says not to taste it. She also finds growing hair out hard.

**Q47 — D)** She ate too much jokbal
먹었어 is past tense and 너무 많이 means 'too much'. She regrets how much jokbal she ate.

**Q48 — B)** 체력
'체력이 떨어지는 느낌' means 'a feeling that my stamina is dropping'. 체력 (stamina) is the thing that can drop.

**Q49 — D)** She starts with 10 minutes at speed 6, then raises it by 0.5 every 5 minutes
She runs lightly at 6 for 10 minutes, then adds 0.5 every 5 minutes. She only goes up to about 12 when pushing hard.

**Q50 — C)** A playful, polite storyteller tone: "so that's how it is"
-답니다 is a polite, slightly cute way to close an explanation. She uses it to wrap up the shoe-size talk.
