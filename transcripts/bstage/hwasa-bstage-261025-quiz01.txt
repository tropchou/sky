# Answer Key

**Q1 — D)** Playfully greeting the viewers in Japanese ("nice to meet you")
하즈메 마시떼 is her pronunciation of Japanese はじめまして, 'nice to meet you', used jokingly at the start. It is a greeting, not a farewell.

**Q2 — D)** It denies or corrects an assumption: 'it's not that...'
She is correcting what viewers might think: 'My hair isn't greasy, okay.' The pattern rejects an assumption and is not a suggestion or a request.

**Q3 — A)** gras et collés
떡지다 describes hair that is greasy and clumped together. She says her hair isn't like that, it's just styling product.

**Q4 — C)** It looks that way because she put styling product in it
She says 'It's just styling product', right after denying that her hair is greasy. So the greasy-days option is the opposite of what she says.

**Q5 — D)** Very formal speech, used jokingly as a mock-formal introduction
-ㅂ니까/-습니까 is the stiff formal style. She uses it jokingly before saying '저는 안혜진이라고 합니다', in contrast to her usual casual 반말.

**Q6 — D)** The spot where she is sitting is comfy
편하다 means comfortable: 'Ah, this spot is comfy.' She only moves later because of the connection trouble.

**Q7 — B)** She is wondering aloud, half to herself, whether it's working
Verb stem + -나? is a casual question of wondering, often self-directed. She is musing about whether the live is coming through.

**Q8 — C)** It uses the honorific -시- and -구요, a polite tone toward the viewers when asking a favor
-어/아 주시다 is the honorific form of 'do for me', and -구요 is polite. She slips into a politer tone when making a request of the fans.

**Q9 — B)** Her voice is shot
목이 셔서 means 'because my voice is hoarse', so she can't sing for them. She even imitates her creaky voice right after.

**Q10 — C)** Ça grince
삐그덕 is a sound word for creaking. She uses it to imitate her hoarse voice: 'Creak, creak, creak.'

**Q11 — B)** 를
챌린지를 보다 means 'to watch the challenge', so the object particle 를 is needed. The line means 'I was watching the challenge videos all day.'

**Q12 — A)** Because she ate way too much the day before
She says she meant to detox today because she overate yesterday ('어제 너무 많이 먹어가지고'). -어 가지고 gives the reason.

**Q13 — A)** 더라고
'진짜 참을 수가 없더라고' means 'Seriously, I just couldn't resist.' -더라고 reports something she experienced and found out firsthand.

**Q14 — C)** 어야지
'진짜 이젠 진짜 안먹어야지' means 'now I'm really not eating anymore.' -어야지 expresses a firm resolution to herself.

**Q15 — B)** She resisted the urge to eat it
참다 means to resist or hold back. She barely resisted going to Ichiran, though she couldn't resist monjayaki.

**Q16 — B)** A feeling of 'I just can't possibly stand it / hold back'
With 못 참겠어, -겠- expresses a spontaneous judgment about her present state: she feels she can't resist. It is not a future plan here.

**Q17 — D)** Monjayaki
She says she resisted Ichiran, but monjayaki she can't resist. Ichiran is the contrast, not the answer.

**Q18 — C)** 고기
'나는 김, 그리고 고기 한 번 더 추가' means 'I add seaweed, and an extra serving of meat.' 고기 means meat.

**Q19 — B)** Whether 'tamago' is the right Japanese word for egg
She says 달걀 and then asks if 'tamago' is correct, and gets '응' (yeah) as an answer. She is unsure of the Japanese word.

**Q20 — C)** Realized
깨닫다 means to realize. 'There's something I realized recently' leads into her discovery about working out in order to eat.

**Q21 — A)** A sudden realization or discovery, said half to oneself
-는구나 marks newly realized information. She just figured out that she works out in order to eat.

**Q22 — A)** She exercises in order to be able to eat
먹기 위해서 means 'in order to eat', so eating is the goal and working out is the means. The wrong options reverse or invent that.

**Q23 — A)** A guess based on how she felt: 'I guess I must have missed you'
-나 보다/-나 봐 is a guess drawn from observation or feeling. She infers that she missed the fans.

**Q24 — D)** 뻔했어
'눈물 날 뻔했어' means 'I almost cried.' -ㄹ 뻔하다 means 'almost did, but didn't'.

**Q25 — B)** Tears came up
She says 'While I was singing I MISS YOU, the tears came.' -다가 marks something happening in the middle of an action.

**Q26 — A)** Her back muscles are covered up, less defined
파묻히다 means to be buried. Here her back muscles are 'buried', meaning less visible right now, not painful.

**Q27 — B)** 가
콘서트가 4천석이었대 uses the subject particle 가 with 이다 ('The concert was 4,000 seats'). 를, 에게, 에서 don't fit that function.

**Q28 — A)** Four years ago it was 4,000 seats, today 8,000
She says the last Japan concert, four years ago, was 4,000 seats, but today was 8,000 and full.

**Q29 — B)** 꽉
꽉 찼다 means 'packed full'. The line means 'and it was full'.

**Q30 — D)** 는데도
'그렇게 공백이 길었는데도' means 'even after being away for so long'. -는데도 means 'even though'.

**Q31 — A)** Cheering us on
응원하다 means to cheer on or support. She is describing the fans cheering MAMAMOO on at the concert.

**Q32 — D)** 도
무무들도 있고 means 'there were even MooMoos (crying)'. 도 adds 'also/even', while 를, 에서 and 로 don't fit with 있다 here.

**Q33 — C)** 이
눈물이 나다 ('tears come') takes the subject particle 이. 나다 is intransitive, so the object particle 를 doesn't fit.

**Q34 — B)** 갑자기
갑자기 means 'suddenly'. 'Suddenly, the tears just came like this.' Tears coming on purpose or slowly doesn't fit.

**Q35 — C)** Because she cried secretly, so they couldn't have seen it
The next line says 'Because I cried secretly.' She teases that of course the viewers didn't see it.

**Q36 — A)** 을
눈물을 흘리다 ('to shed tears') needs the object particle 을. The line means 'Because I cried secretly.'

**Q37 — B)** 으니까
-(으)니까 gives the reason: she cried in secret, so of course they didn't see. 는데도 would mean 'even though', which doesn't fit.

**Q38 — C)** 애틋했어
애틋하다 means tender or fond. The Japanese MooMoos' gaze was warm, not cold or scary.

**Q39 — D)** Vous, vous ne faites pas le poids.
Here 안 돼 is a teasing 'you can't compete' compared with the Japanese MooMoos. It is not a ban, and it is not praise.

**Q40 — C)** Step it up, make more effort
분발하다 means to redouble your effort. It is a playful scolding: the viewers should try harder.

**Q41 — A)** 거만해
거만하다 means arrogant or cocky: 'You guys are way too cocky.' 겸손하다 (humble) is the opposite.

**Q42 — B)** Echoing a chat comment as a question and reacting to it
She repeats what a commenter wrote as an echo question: 'You cried during So Cute? The friend next to you?' The crying is the commenter's, not hers.

**Q43 — D)** J'ai l'impression de m'hypnotiser moi-même.
최면을 걸다 means to hypnotize. She feels she is hypnotizing herself to just live cute despite the hardships.

**Q44 — A)** Even though / despite
'Even though things are really tough' shows contrast. It is not a cause, a purpose or a timing.

**Q45 — A)** Rough, tough
험난하다 describes a hard, rugged path or situation. She contrasts it with deciding to live cute anyway.

**Q46 — D)** 잖아
'막 지칠 때가 있잖아' means 'there are times when I get worn out, you know.' -잖아 appeals to shared knowledge.

**Q47 — C)** 지칠
지치다 means to get worn out. She says there are times on stage when she gets exhausted.

**Q48 — C)** Fresh air, a refreshing change
환기 is ventilation or airing out, and here it means a refreshing break. Singing So Cute feels like a breath of fresh air.

**Q49 — B)** Let's push through it
헤치다/헤쳐나가다 means to push through obstacles. -아/어 보자 makes it 'let's give it a try'.

**Q50 — D)** Prohibition: it must not happen
-아/어서는 안 되다 means 'must not'. She says this live must not stop.
